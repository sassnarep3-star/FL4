Validation gates

Fifteen gates, each aimed at one specific way luck masquerades as skill in a betting record.

These gates are documentation until SG-09 makes them code
Fifteen gates, each aimed at one specific way a lucky or overfitted strategy can look like skill. Two design choices do most of the work: they fail closed (a missing threshold, a missing journal entry or an unknown publication timestamp stops promotion rather than allowing it), and they are rank-agnostic - a gate never says 'good enough to try', only pass, fail or not-run. 8 threshold cells are blank by policy because they encode risk appetite rather than a measurement (F-05).

Why CLV, and not the scoreboard, is the promotion metric

A strategy's profit is the sum of its edge and its luck, and for a few hundred bets the second term is usually bigger. Closing-line value is the part of the trade you can audit the next day: did we get a better price than the market's final, most-informed price? It is imperfect - it measures price-beating, not long-run profit, and it depends on the anchor book being genuinely efficient, which is why the anchor gets fixed by policy before the season rather than chosen afterwards by whoever flatters the result.

The consequence for the brief's 'remove luck' requirement is concrete: a strategy with good PnL and bad CLV is killed, and a strategy with bad PnL and good CLV is kept under review. That is the opposite of how most betting records are judged, and it is the only version that can distinguish a process from a run of coin flips. The tie-break policy is pre-registered in Q-MODEL-06 so nobody decides it after seeing the money.
