Depth & deficiency

What the official data can say about depleted units - and the boundary past which this site refuses to guess.

The honest limit of this page
The brief asks the system to know when 2nd/3rd/4th-string backups replace starters. The official report has no replacement field, so this page states who is unavailable and which unit it hits and deliberately does not name who fills in. Naming a replacement would require a depth-chart source, and none is verified in this environment ([F-02](flagged.html), [Q-ROSTER-01](questions.html)). A wrong 'next man up' is worse than a blank, because it silently propagates into every price built on it.

What the availability data shows for Week 10

Defensive front depletion. 6 clubs are missing two or more players listed as DE/DT/LB (IND 3, HOU 2, JAX 2, KC 2, MIN 2, TEN 2). That is where an availability count is most misleading, because two reserve linemen and two starting interior defenders produce the same number here and very different football.

Offensive line. 15 clubs carry at least one lineman (T/G/C) Out or Doubtful, and 3 carry two or more (AZ 2, NO 2, SEA 2). This is the unit the totals and rush-volume ideas in S-02 target, and it is also the unit where a club can plausibly lose a starter and his next two backups inside one week - which is exactly the 'how far down the chart do we fall' question the source cannot answer.

Not everything missing is injured. 3 rows in this snapshot are explicitly 'Not injury related' (rest or personal matter) while still carrying an Out game status: JAX/Jessie Bates, JAX/Jordan Davis, HOU/Azeez Al-Shaair. A model that books those as lost quality for the following week double-counts a decision made to preserve quality, so the rest flag must be a field of its own before any counting is trusted.

Roster reality moves underneath the model. The same page carries mid-season personnel changes: a linebacker now listed by a different club after a trade, a franchise quarterback placed on injured reserve with a first-time starter described as 'in line', and a starting wide receiver described as 'likely' to go on IR while the report gives him no game status at all. None of those three is an event the weekly report alone delivers, and all three change what a depth chain looks like.

Unit assignment is a mechanical mapping of the position code printed on the official page (T/G/C to offensive line, DE/DT to the front seven, and so on). It is arithmetic on verified rows, not an opinion about any player's quality. Starter-or-backup status is not knowable from this source, which is why no column claims it.

How the deficiency model is meant to work once a depth source exists

The quantity worth having is not 'how many are out' but 'how far down the depth chart does the unit fall, and how much quality is lost at each step'. That is three inputs: a per-unit depth chain with availability flags on each link, a replacement-value estimate per position, and the snaps each link was projected to take.

Of those three, one exists in this repo (availability). The other two need sources that are not verified here. So the pipeline ships with the arithmetic ready and the coefficients blank: the position ladder in SG-05 is explicitly labelled estimated, unvalidated, and gate G-7 exists to test whether any strategy survives when those estimates are perturbed - which is the same as asking whether the trade is about football or about guessing the injury report correctly.

Until a depth feed exists, the defensible use of this page is as a risk register: which clubs are playing with a visibly depleted unit this week, and therefore which markets should a model distrust its season-long priors on. That use needs no invented numbers.
