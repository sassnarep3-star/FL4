Leaderboard

A competition designed so that the winner is the strategy with the most reproducible price advantage - not the loudest story.

The board has zero entries
A leaderboard is a record of results, and there are no results: no prices, no bets, no settlements. This page therefore publishes the competition rules in a form that can be audited and argued with before anything is staked on it, and the table stays empty. A populated table of plausible ranks would be the most dangerous object in this repository, because it is the kind of artefact people screenshot.

Scoring

Primary - Luck-adjusted CLV score. score = (mean CLV in cents per bet) x sqrt(n) / (1 + overfit_penalty), where overfit_penalty = log(1 + variants_tried_in_family) from the decision journal, and n is capped at the sample gate.

It rewards a real, reproducible price advantage and punishes a family in which many variants were tried to find it. It can be computed the day a bet settles, which is the point.

Secondary - Risk-adjusted growth. log-growth of a fractional-Kelly bankroll on the realised results, reported only after the sample gate passes; displayed greyed with 'not yet significant' before then.

Money is the final argument, but it is a slow and noisy argument; showing it without the significance marker invites exactly the luck-chasing the brief forbids.

Tie-breakers, in order: Stability across sub-periods (lowest variance of the primary metric across weekly blocks); Robustness score from G-7 (fraction of edge retained under input perturbation); Capacity at target stake from G-4; never: recency of a hot streak. The last line of that list is the load-bearing one.

The overfit penalty reads its trial count from the decision journal, so 'we only publish the winner' becomes impossible by construction: a family where eight variants were tried is scored as if eight were tried. That single mechanic is what makes this a skill contest rather than a beauty contest for noise.

Promotion rules

  • A strategy may not skip a tier and may not be promoted inside an in-progress week.
  • Promotion is decided by the gate runner's output file, not by a person reading a page; a person may only veto, never override upward.
  • Re-registering a killed strategy under a new id is a G-14 violation; the journal records the family so the rename is visible.
  • A strategy whose required input is unavailable is not 'failing' - it stays at Tier 0 with blocked_on populated, and the block is surfaced on the questions page so it is a procurement task rather than a scoreboard loss.
