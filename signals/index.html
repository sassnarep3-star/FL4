Executive summary

Objective. Originate NFL lines rather than chase them, treat strategy development as a ranked competition, and make every availability and depth fact legible enough to decide on. That objective is now expressed as 38 traced requirements, 16 candidate strategies, 15 gates, an 11-pair correlation register and a live, source-linked data layer.

What was built on top of an empty repository. At session start this repo held one file with five bytes of README. Everything here is net-new: a verification policy, 11 machine-readable datasets, a generator, 14 pages, a CI deploy, and the coordination board for other agent sessions.

What is verified and usable today. The Week 10, 2026 availability report is captured from the league's own page for 28 clubs - 81 players Out or Doubtful with position, disclosed injury, practice participation and game status - plus all 32 club records, the Week 10 fixture list and the played Thursday result. Build-time checks re-reconcile the row-level data against the declared team totals, the standings against the matchup records, and the fixture list against the snapshot, and this capture against a second session's independent read of the same page - which is how one transcription omission (a Packers linebacker listed Out) was found and corrected rather than left standing. Every check below runs on each build.

What the data already tells a decision-maker. Availability is not evenly distributed: 6 clubs carry five or more Out/Doubtful designations (IND 6, DAL 6, MIN 5, GB 5, TEN 5, HOU 5) while 3 carry none (LV, NYG, PIT). Two rows on the Jaguars' report are marked 'Not injury related - resting player' yet still carry an Out game status, which is a direct warning that counting designations confuses rest with loss of quality. A Seahawks quarterback ruled Out is corroborated by an independent league article naming his replacement as starter, while a Dolphins injured-reserve move appears only in a headline and produces no weekly row at all - so a report-only pipeline is structurally blind to exactly the changes that redefine a team's depth for weeks.

What is missing, and it is one thing. Market prices. There is no odds or line feed in this environment, so nothing that depends on a price can be measured: not closing-line value, not backtests, not correlation of settled legs, not originated lines. The brief's central ask is therefore not delivered as a number - it is delivered as an architecture plus a named, single blocking decision (Q-MKT-01), which is the honest state.

The depth requirement needs a second source. 'Know when 2nd/3rd/4th-string backups replace starters' cannot come from the official report, which has no replacement field. This site publishes who is unavailable and refuses to name a replacement, because inventing one would corrupt the very signals it is meant to produce. The fix is a depth or transactions feed (Q-ROSTER-01, SG-03).

How luck is removed. A strategy is judged primarily on closing-line value per bet (measurable the next day), never on win rate over a short sample; it must survive walk-forward folds, a sample floor of 500 bets across 250+ distinct games, a multiple-testing correction read from a journal of failed variants, an input-perturbation robustness test, and a hard no-look-ahead timestamp rule. Strategies that cannot pass stay published with the block named - they are not deleted, and they are not promoted.

Governance. No capital is exposed. The leaderboard is empty by design, rewards are non-cash, and the reviewer's only required action is to answer 18 blocking questions and set 5 numeric thresholds. Suggestions are held at 'proposed' until dispositioned; silence never approves.

The six decisions that unblock the most work

  • Q-MKT-01 - choose a price source (vendor, exchange, or self-capture). Everything quantitative is behind this one.
  • Q-RISK-01/02 - state the bankroll policy and staking fraction, or confirm research-only mode.
  • Q-ROSTER-01 - pick the depth-chart authority, or accept that the site names absences but not replacements.
  • Q-MODEL-01/02 - confirm CLV as the primary metric and the sample floor, which fixes what 'skill' means here.
  • Q-LEGAL-01/02 - confirm jurisdiction/operator legality and data redistribution terms before any live exposure.
  • Q-GOV-01/02 - name the approver and define what silence means for suggestions.

Each is reproduced on the Questions page with the known/unknown/resolve columns filled in, so answering it is a short reply rather than a research task.
